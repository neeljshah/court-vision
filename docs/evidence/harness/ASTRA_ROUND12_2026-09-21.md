2. THE CADENCE QUESTION

Choose fewer markets at five seconds. Broad 60-second bulk quotes remain useful context, but 1,200 individual ladders every 60 seconds require 20 requests/second before tape or state.

Allocate the sustainable request budget B:

- 45%: five-second books for selected live winner tickers.
- 35%: trade polling and pagination; unfinished pagination takes priority over new admissions.
- 10%: complete event-set bulk snapshots, targeting 30-60 seconds.
- 5%: pregame discovery/quotes every five minutes, tightening near scheduled start.
- 5%: retries and headroom.

Capacity for individual books is floor(5 x 0.45 x B): about two to six tickers at B=1-3. Reserve slots across predefined sport strata, select by deterministic rotation before games, and hold selection throughout the observation window. Do not rank games by realized activity. Derivative ladders receive spare capacity only after book and tape obligations are met.

State requests also need explicit provider budgets; include them in B if the stated ceiling covers all providers.

First 48 hours: measure per-ticker median/p95/max cadence, request latency, throttling, pages per drain, watermark lag, gap duration, state age, linkage success, touch-size coverage, qualified games/day and synchronization skew. Count unchanged snapshots as successful capture. Reduce admissions when deadlines fail.

4. DERIVATIVE COHERENCE

First validate settlement definitions, completeness, tick grids and common payout units. Bulk retrieval does not guarantee atomic snapshots. Require receipt skew <=5 seconds for adjudication; otherwise report synchronization failure.

For each contract define:
L = bid minus applicable fee minus one tick;
U = ask plus applicable fee plus one tick.

Use actual series-specific, quantity-specific fees and rounding. Pin the dated schedule; its current text and tables require reconciliation before fractional-fee certification. [Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)

Three cheap falsifiers:

- Complete sets: for mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcomes, require sum(L) <=1<=sum(U). Include draws where applicable; arbitrary spread/total ladders are not complete sets.
- Nested totals: if high-threshold success implies low-threshold success, flag L_high>U_low.
- Spread/winner consistency: a positive-margin cover implies winning under matching settlement rules; flag L_cover>U_winner. Equivalent propositions require overlapping intervals.

Report residual size, persistence and game incidence as market-structure measurements. Related contracts from the same games do not automatically establish independent G6 families.

5. THREE BIGGEST DEADLINE RISKS

- Laptop interruption: sleep, reboot, storage exhaustion or network loss breaks continuity. Cheapest mitigation: OS-managed restart, plugged-in capture, bounded shards, free-space checks, and watchdog checks of actual archive growth.
- Silent incompleteness: healthy processes can retain stale cursors or mismatched state. Mitigation: watermark-progress alarms, explicit gaps, real-row fixtures and daily score-blind qualification.
- Calendar and family shortfall: MLB cannot sustain November-December collection; tennis availability and derivatives may also narrow. Mitigation: establish two eligible, independently justified families now, reserve capture capacity, and maintain a weekly denominator forecast. November 5 is the latest start only if instruments and family definitions are already qualified. An interruption must not be hidden by stitching weeks.
