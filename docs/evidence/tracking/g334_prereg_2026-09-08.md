## 3. Court template geometry, in feet

Contract: `x` is the court WIDTH, 0 to 50; `y` is the court LENGTH, 0 to 94. This is the
`g196_homography_from_labelled_corners` / `g252_projection_accuracy_in_pixels` axis contract and is
NOT the transposed `keypoint_calib.CANONICAL_LANDMARKS` one. NBA dimensions: length 94, width 50,
lane 16 wide by 19 deep, free-throw and centre circles radius 6, three-point radius 23.75 about a
basket centre at (25, 5.25), corner-three straights 3 ft inside each sideline.

Because a basketball broadcast frame shows one end, the fitted template is the NEAR HALF with the
visible baseline at `y = 0`. The polylines, sampled at 1 ft spacing for scoring:

    baseline        (0,0)-(50,0)
    sideline left   (0,0)-(0,47)          sideline right  (50,0)-(50,47)
    half-court line (0,47)-(50,47)
    lane left       (17,0)-(17,19)        lane right      (33,0)-(33,19)
    free-throw line (17,19)-(33,19)
    free-throw circle   centre (25,19) radius 6, full circle
    centre circle       centre (25,47) radius 6, the y <= 47 half only
    corner-three left   (3,0)-(3,14.198)  corner-three right  (47,0)-(47,14.198)
    three-point arc     centre (25,5.25) radius 23.75, between the two corner-three ends

14.198 is 5.25 + sqrt(23.75^2 - 22^2), where the corner-three straights meet the arc. Arcs are
approximated by their 1 ft chord sampling and are never fitted as conics in this row.

Which end of the court the frame shows is NOT decided. G242 measured that the wrong hoop end is
accepted 89/89 by the production matcher, and no automatic end discriminator exists. Court
coordinates are therefore reported in the near-end convention; metrics (c) and (d) of section 8 are
invariant under the reflection `y -> 94 - y`, so this choice cannot move them.

## 4. Line extraction -- the parameters, all sealed

Reused read-only, not rewritten: `scripts/platformkit/g123_low_contrast_lines.enhance_contrast`
(CIELAB-L CLAHE, clipLimit 2.0, tiles 8x8), `domains/basketball/tracking/line_calibration`
`detect_lsd_segments` (grayscale OpenCV LSD) and `candidate_line_group_details`, and
`scripts/platformkit/g132_additive_candidate_union.union_segments`. Composition follows
`g210_court_model_fit_to_lines._groups`: raw LSD on the frame, unioned with LSD on the
contrast-enhanced frame, then collinear grouping.

    ARM A parameter set (the first design; every value fixed now)
      LSD_MIN_LEN      28     minimum segment length in measurement-image pixels
      GROUP_ANGLE_DEG   5.0   collinear grouping angle tolerance
      GROUP_OFFSET_PX  18.0   collinear grouping offset tolerance
      FAMILY_TOL_DEG   35     a group joins a family only within this of the family mode
      TOP_K             7     groups kept per family, by observed support length
      TAU               6.0   soft-chamfer half-width in measurement-image pixels
      SCORE_MIN         0.30  minimum mean soft-chamfer score density to accept a frame
      MIN_SCORED       60     minimum template sample points inside the image
      PX_PER_FT_RANGE  [4, 60]  plausible median local scale
      QUAD_AREA_MIN    0.02   projected half-court quad area as a share of the image
      WINDOW            5     frames per shot window, centred on the evaluation index
      CUT_L1            0.20  the route's own `_SIFT_CUT_THRESH`, read not invented

FAMILY SPLIT: segment angles are taken modulo 180 degrees and binned into 5-degree bins weighted by
segment length. The two family modes are the two largest bins separated by at least 20 degrees; a
group joins the nearer mode when within FAMILY_TOL_DEG of it and is discarded otherwise. The two
families correspond to the court's two orthogonal directions: family W holds the lines of constant
`y` (baseline, free-throw line, half-court line) and family L the lines of constant `x` (sidelines,
lane sides, corner-three straights). Which family is which is decided by the score, not asserted.

## 5. The hypothesis set and its scoring -- where this differs from G210 and G210b

G210 measured 0/17 and G210b, with the group cap removed and 16,384 hypotheses per frame, measured
0/17 again; G210b's memo records that the correct groups were present in the pool on all 17 frames
and that no uniform draw ever contained them. G217 measured that the solver and the court model
contribute 0.000000 px of that error. The one design change this row makes is therefore to the
SAMPLER, and to nothing else: the hypothesis set is enumerated COMPLETELY over a structured space
instead of drawn uniformly over an unstructured one.

    court value candidates   family W: y in {0, 19, 47}
                             family L: x in {0, 3, 17, 33, 47, 50}
    hypothesis  = (two family-W image groups, two family-L image groups,
                   an assignment of court values to each, both orders tried)
    count       = C(TOP_K,2)^2 x 3 x 2 x 15 x 2 = 21 x 21 x 180 = 79,380 per frame at TOP_K 7,
                  enumerated exhaustively; there is no random draw anywhere in the search.

Each hypothesis intersects its two W groups with its two L groups to give four image points whose
court coordinates are known, and `cv2.getPerspectiveTransform` gives the candidate image-to-court
`H`. This is `g196.solve_homography` and `line_calibration.solve_from_lines` arithmetic, unchanged.

SCORING (fit half only): the FIT segments are rasterised one pixel wide into a mask and
`cv2.distanceTransform(255 - mask, cv2.DIST_L2, 3)` is taken once per frame. Every template sample
point of section 3 is projected into the frame through `inv(H)`; points outside the image are
dropped; the score is the mean over the surviving points of `max(0, 1 - d / TAU)` where `d` is the
distance-transform lookup. The best-scoring hypothesis wins. This is the same line-support idea as
`g210._support`, computed against a distance transform rather than a per-group angle-and-offset test.

VALIDITY GATE, applied to the winner before a frame counts as valid: `H` finite and invertible; at
least MIN_SCORED surviving sample points; score density at least SCORE_MIN; the projected 50 x 47
half-court quad convex, finite, and at least QUAD_AREA_MIN of the image area; and the median local
scale inside PX_PER_FT_RANGE. The quad clauses descend from `g210._valid_projection`. G247 measured
that quad shape alone does NOT separate a correct court from a wrong one, so this is a sanity gate
that removes degenerate fits, never a correctness test, and it is not reported as one.

## 6. Temporal -- per-shot median

For each evaluation index `i` the frames `i-2 .. i+2` are decoded (WINDOW 5). A 32-bin grayscale
histogram L1 difference at the route's own CUT_L1 threshold splits that window into runs; only the
run containing `i` is kept, and that run is this row's "shot". The reported `H` for evaluation frame
`i` is the elementwise MEDIAN, over the frames of that run that passed the section 5 validity gate,
of `H / H[2,2]`. A median rather than an exponential average: over at most five matrices it is the
simpler and more robust of the two and needs no decay constant. If no frame of the run is valid, the
evaluation frame is invalid and is counted as such.

Stated in advance: G242, G244, G247 and G248 each measured, on the same 89 frames, that no available
hand-built automatic signal separates a correct court from a wrong one. The per-shot median will
therefore smooth a wrong `H` exactly as willingly as a right one. It suppresses single-frame noise
and nothing more, and no claim beyond that is made from it.

## 7. Held-out split rule (B8)

SEED: 334. For a section stem `s` and evaluation index `i` the generator is
`numpy.random.default_rng(int.from_bytes(sha256(("%s|%d|334" % (s, i)).encode("ascii")).digest()[:8], "big"))`.
It permutes the detected segment indices of that frame; the first `ceil(n/2)` are the FIT half and
the rest the HELD-OUT half. Every frame in a shot window uses its own centre index `i`, so the split
is fixed by the sealed seed and by nothing measured.

The search of section 5 sees the FIT half ONLY: the grouping, the family split, the TOP_K choice and
the distance transform are all built from FIT segments alone. The HELD-OUT half is never touched
until the winner is scored.

## 8. The metrics and their denominators

Per section and per arm, in the measurement image of section 2 and in feet for (c) and (d):

  (a) HELD-OUT LINE REPROJECTION, the bar cell. A second distance transform is built from the
      HELD-OUT segments alone. Metric (a) is the MEDIAN, over the template sample points that fall
      inside the image, of the distance in measurement-image pixels to the nearest held-out line
      pixel. n is reported twice: the number of scored sample points and the number of held-out
      segments. A companion column `heldout_rev_px` reports the opposite direction -- the median
      over points sampled at 1 px along the held-out segments of the distance to the nearest
      projected template line -- with its own n. The reverse direction is penalised by crowd and
      graphics clutter and the forward direction is helped by it; both are printed so the reader can
      see both failure directions. ONLY the forward column is the bar.
  (b) VALID-H SHARE: evaluation frames whose per-shot median `H` passed the gate, over 40.
  (c) FEET INSIDE THE COURT: the person detector (YOLOv8n, `classes=[0]`, `conf=0.3`, `imgsz=640`,
      CPU) runs ONCE per evaluation frame and its foot points, from
      `g330_panorama_identity.feet_from_boxes`, are SHARED by both arms exactly as G330 shared them.
      A foot counts only in a frame where THAT arm returned an `H`, so the denominator is per arm.
      Inside means the projected court point satisfies `0 <= x <= 50` and `0 <= y <= 94`. Feet
      detected over the 40 evaluation frames is printed once per section.
  (d) PLAUSIBILITY: `nn_median_ft` is the median, over evaluation frames carrying at least two
      projected feet, of that frame's median nearest-neighbour distance in feet; n is the number of
      contributing pairs. Under this row's definitions the "fraction of players inside the 94 x 50
      rectangle" IS metric (c): the court polygon of (c) and the rectangle of (d) are the same set.
      It is therefore computed once and reported once, in the `feet_inside` column, and is not
      duplicated under a second denominator.

Every integer cell in the CSV files is zero-padded to six digits and every share is written as a
fraction. Pixel and feet medians are written with three decimals.

## 9. The baseline arm -- the route, scored on the same frames

ARM ROUTE is the production path, scored with metrics (a) to (d) identically, on the same
evaluation frames, the same measurement image and the same shared foot points. Its homography to
court feet is the composition the route itself applies at `unified_pipeline.py:3084` and
`:2736-2737`:

    H_route = SWAP @ diag(94/map_w, 50/map_h, 1) @ M1 @ M @ inv(R)

`M` is the value returned by the unmodified `UnifiedPipeline._get_homography`
(`src/pipeline/unified_pipeline.py:1231-1354`), `M1` is `resources/Rectify1.npy`, `map_w` and
`map_h` are `resources/2d_map.png`, `R` is the section 2 crop-and-scale into the measurement image,
and SWAP exchanges the axes because the route's `ft_x` is the LENGTH and its `ft_y` the WIDTH while
section 3's `x` is the width. The arm is a `UnifiedPipeline.__new__` instance carrying exactly the
attributes that method and its drift check read, built by `g330_attempt2.make_arm`, and EVERY
decoded frame of the section is fed to it in order after the TOPCUT crop, so the route's own
sampling counter, cut gate and EMA tiers decide where a fit happens, as in production. No threshold
is touched: `_H_MIN_INLIERS` 5, the bootstrap 3, `_H_RESET_INLIERS` 40, the Lowe ratio 0.7 and the
RANSAC reprojection threshold 5.0 are read from the route, never set. No panorama cache path is
written and nothing under `src/` is edited to obtain this arm.

## 14. Honest limitations -- stated here, not discovered later

- No ground-truth court coordinates exist anywhere in this row and none are created. Every metric is
  self-consistency or physical plausibility. Neither is accuracy.
- G233 measured 82.18 pct of projected feet inside 94 x 50 ft on a map that FAILS the eye check,
  because the count includes officials, bench and spectators. Metric (c) can therefore pass on a
  wrong map, and a high (c) is never read as a correct map.
- G254 measured that a distance-transform objective walks a correct homography into a wrong one that
  scores better, and that the basin is narrow. Metric (a) is that same family of objective. The
  held-out split controls fitting to particular segments; it does NOT control the whole template
  landing on the wrong set of lines.
- G210 and G210b measured 0/17 for this architecture under uniform sampling and G210b's oracle,
  which assigns roles perfectly, reached only 1/17. Complete enumeration is the single change and it
  is not a guarantee of anything.
- Broadcast zoom-ins and replays will fail line extraction. They CANNOT be labelled automatically:
  G242 measured 89/89 acceptance including close-ups, replays, graphics and the wrong hoop end, and
  G244 measured that the two known cuts fall inside the ordinary single-frame match-count range. The
  `too_few_groups` bucket of section 15 is a PROXY for them and is reported as a proxy, never as a
  count of replays.
- Arcs are approximated by 1 ft chords, never fitted as conics.
- S1 is an Australian NBL broadcast and is played on a FIBA-dimension floor, 28 x 15 m, which is
  91.86 x 49.21 ft. It is scored against the NBA template, so a 2.3 pct length and 1.6 pct width
  mismatch is present in S1 by construction. It is reported, not corrected, and S1 is the reason the
  three added sections were restricted to `nba__`.
- Six sections are a screening sample, not the programme. No claim in this row reaches beyond these
  six sections.

## 15. Failure buckets and their denominators

Counted per section over the 40 evaluation frames, mutually exclusive, in this order:

the PX_PER_FT_RANGE clause alone), `valid`. Their sum is 40 in every section.
