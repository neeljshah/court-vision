# G162 + G163: why the one gate-reaching tennis table fails, and it is not calibration

Contract: [VERIFIER_CONTRACT.md](VERIFIER_CONTRACT.md), sections A (A2, A7) and
B. Measured by the orchestrator on 2026-09-03 while the codex backend returned
`404` and no lane could run. **Diagnosis only. No threshold, bar, gate,
adapter, harness or verdict changed.**

## The table

`tennis_smoke`, on the pod at `/workspace/nba-ai-system/data/tracking/tennis_smoke/`.
It is the orchestrator's hand-run bootstrap of the tennis adapter on the local
reference clip, not a daemon product. Harness verdict:

    tennis_smoke rows=1861 passed=False failures=['duplicate frame-track rows 4',

It reaches the gate and its `calibration_provenance=solved` share is 1,558/1,861
= 0.8372, so **calibration is not what stops it**.

## The mechanism, from the code

`domains/tennis/tracking/identity.py:12-25`, `assign_epoch`, DOES associate
across frames: with two prior centroids it compares the direct pairing against
the crossed pairing by summed L2 distance and keeps the cheaper one. Ids are
minted as `base + 1` and `base + 2`.

The continuity is **scoped to an epoch**. `_end_track_ids`
(`domains/tennis/tracking/adapter.py:172-174`) calls `end_epoch`, which advances
`_track_id_base`, so every subsequent frame gets *fresh ids that can never
associate back*. It is called from two places:

- `adapter.py:142`, inside `_reset`: `self._corners = self._homography = None; self._end_track_ids()` -- i.e. **whenever calibration is lost**;
- `adapter.py:220-229`, on `detect_cut` -- i.e. **on every shot cut**.

Ids run 1..586 with no gaps and are allocated two per epoch, which implies
**293 epochs across 726 emitted frames: an epoch ends roughly every 2.5 emitted
frames.** That is the diagnosis. Association works; it is reset so often that it
never accumulates. On broadcast tennis, cuts are frequent and calibration is
intermittent, and each event mints a new identity pair.

So the answer to G162's spec question (c) is neither of the two options it
offered: association exists AND the frames are gappy, but the dominant cause is a
third thing, epoch churn.

## G163: the jump statistic is NOT degenerate, which falsifies my own hypothesis

I specced G163 expecting `jump_max 48.93` to be an artefact computed over a
handful of pairs, on the reasoning that a median track length of 1 leaves almost
no consecutive same-track observations. **That is wrong.** Counted directly:

- consecutive same-track pairs, players only: **844**
- consecutive same-track pairs, all classes: **1,275**

844 pairs is a real denominator, not a degenerate one (B9 does not fire). So
`jump_max` is a genuine measurement and the three harness failures are not simply
one failure wearing three hats. The honest reading is that the ~2.9 pct of tracks
lasting 11+ frames supply most of the pairs.

## G163: the four duplicates

The four duplicate `(frame, track_id)` keys are all **track id 99**, at frames
**5676, 5679, 5688, 5691**. One track, four frames, not four scattered defects.
Whether they are byte-identical rows or two positions claiming one key is NOT
established here, and neither is the code path that admits them despite the
adapter's one-detection-per-court-half rule.

## An anomaly worth naming

G152b measured the same local reference clip at **28,773 decoded frames and
2,597 distinct emitted frames**. This pod run of what should be the same source
emitted **726** distinct frames. Two runs of one clip differing by 3.6x in
emitted frames is not explained here and should not be waved away as run-to-run
noise.

## What is NOT claimed and NOT verified

- No fix is proposed and none should be inferred. `min_players`, the two-slot
  rule, `jump_max`, the coverage bar and the coordinate contract are all frozen,
  and G148 already established that relaxing the two-slot rule cannot raise
  coverage as the harness computes it.
- The harness's own pairing for `jump_max` was not re-derived; 844 and 1,275 are
  my counts of consecutive same-track observations, not a reproduction of the
  harness's internal statistic. `jump_max 48.93` itself is quoted from the
  harness, not recomputed.
- The duplicate rows were not opened; their byte content and admitting code path
  are unmeasured.
- No eye check was performed. G162 and G163 both require rendered frames, and
  rendering was not done here. **Both rows therefore remain OPEN for their eye
  checks**; this memo is the measurement half only.
- The 726-versus-2,597 discrepancy is unexplained.
- Whether epoch churn is dominated by `detect_cut` or by calibration loss is
  unmeasured: the two call sites were read but not instrumented, so their
  relative contribution to the 293 epochs is unknown.
