# PROPOSED ROW B -- 85 pct of the corpus has no timebase provenance, and the jump bar is applied to intervals that differ by 25 pct

**Measured state.** `adapter_run.py:128-129` writes `sampling` and
`timebase_metrics` into each report. **Only 28 of 187 reports (15.0 pct) carry a
`sampling` block; 159 of 187 (85.0 pct) carry none**, so for those 159 the
stride, the source fps and the wall-clock spacing of consecutive observations are
unrecorded. Among the 28 that do carry one:

| quantity | measured distribution (n = 28 reports) |
|---|---|
| stride | 3 (14), 2 (8), 6 (6) |
| `sample_interval_seconds` | 0.1001 (11), 0.1000 (9), 0.0800 (7), 0.08342 (1) |
| `source_frame_rate` | 30.0 (8), 25.0 (7), 29.97 (6), 59.94 (5), 60.0 (1), 23.976 (1) |

Interval range 0.0800 s to 0.1001 s = a **25.1 pct spread**. The stride is not a
constant and never was: `scripts/platformkit/tracking_timebase.py:30-36` sets
`stride = max(1, int(round(frame_rate * 0.1)))`, and falls back to
`SamplingPlan(None, 3, 0.1, None)` when the fps is unreadable -- stride 3 with
`sample_interval_seconds = None`, so `per_second()` returns None and the clip
silently gets no rate conversion.

**Why it matters.** `tracking_timebase.py:58` states the rail plainly:
"per_second values are reporting-only; frozen harness gates use raw per-step
values". The single 8.0 ft `jump_p95` bar is therefore applied to a raw per-step
displacement whose wall-clock interval varies by 25.1 pct across the clips that
record it and is **unknown for 159 of 187**. Two clips can fail or pass the same
bar at physical speeds that differ by a quarter, and no artifact records which is
which. This is the shared root of the G34 denominator complaint and the G38 fps
complaint: both memos had to guess a timebase the report format already has a
slot for.

**Evidence.** `data/tracking_reports/*/*.json` (the 28/159 split and both
histograms), `scripts/platformkit/tracking_timebase.py:30-36,48-59`,
`scripts/platformkit/adapter_run.py:96-99,128-129`. The stride-2-at-25-fps
behaviour is already pinned by an existing test,
`scripts/platformkit/test_tracking_timebase.py:6,10`
(`assert slow.stride == 2 and slow.sample_interval_seconds == 0.08`).

**Single next experiment.** Backfill the `sampling` block for the 159 reports
that lack it by re-probing each clip's source header and recording, per report,
whether the fps was readable -- then report the count of reports whose stride was
the `frame_rate is None` fallback 3 rather than an fps-derived stride. Read-only
backfill into a NEW field; do not overwrite any existing field and do not move
the 8.0 bar.

# PROPOSED ROW C -- G38's 30 fps premise is FALSIFIED in detail; its verdict survives, its numbers must be restated

**Measured state.** G38 wrote its ft/s arithmetic on "stride 3 and 30 fps ... so
0.1 s" (`g38_tennis_jump_diagnosis_2026-09-02.md:72`) and listed the assumption
in its own NOT VERIFIED section. Measured today: **of the 8 tennis reports on the
pod that record a source frame rate, 7 are 25.000 fps and 1 is 23.976 fps.
None is 30 fps.** All 8 carry stride 2 and `sample_interval_seconds` 0.0800 or
0.08342.

G38's four clips (`tennis_02` to `tennis_05`) are in the other bucket: they carry
**no `sampling` block at all**, so their fps was never recorded, and their source
videos are no longer in `data/footage_corpus/` (the corpus holds
`tennis__tennis_06` through `_10`, `_3x3eEWCZmWQ`, `_459iho5_AFs`,
`_nyYk2nPZAwY` and `_nyYk2nPZAwY_720p` only). Their sampling can therefore be
recovered only from the tables. Measured modal inter-frame step, denominator =
distinct frames present per table:

| clip | distinct frames | modal step | modal fraction | distinct step values | max frame |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| tennis_02 | 1,951 | 3 | 0.4877 | 63 | 28,767 |
| tennis_03 | 3,392 | 3 | 0.6511 | 58 | 28,236 |
| tennis_04 | 3,789 | 3 | 0.7344 | 52 | 28,770 |
| tennis_05 | 2,905 | 3 | 0.6463 | 54 | 23,994 |

So stride 3 is right for those four -- consistent with the pre-timebase code path
whose hardcoded default is 3 (`src/pipeline/unified_pipeline.py:377`) -- but
0.1 s per step is right only if the source was 30 fps, and every tennis source
this program has actually measured is 25 fps.

**Consequence, stated honestly.** At 25 fps a stride-3 step is **0.12 s**, not
0.10 s. G38's "10 to 29 ft implies 100-290 ft/s" becomes **83-242 ft/s**. Against
the ~39 ft/s sprint reference that is still one to two orders of magnitude beyond
human motion, so **G38's conclusion -- an unstable per-half player selection, not
a metric artifact -- is unchanged**. What is wrong is the published number, by
20 pct, in the direction that makes the defect look worse than it is.

**Evidence.** `data/tracking_reports/tennis/*.json` on the pod (the 8 fps values);
the frame-step table above from `/tmp/g35_probe_out.json` over
`data/tracking/tennis_0{2,3,4,5}/tracking_data.csv`;
`docs/evidence/tracking/g38_tennis_jump_diagnosis_2026-09-02.md:72,119-121`;
`scripts/platformkit/tracking_timebase.py:30-36`.

**Why it matters.** A ft/s figure quoted from a memo whose fps was assumed is not
a measurement. Two other tennis rows (G34 section 2, and any future G38 fix) sit
on the same assumption.

**Single next experiment.** Re-probe the sources for `tennis_02` to `tennis_05`
-- they are absent from the corpus, so the honest outcome may be
CLOSED AT LIMIT (fps unrecoverable) -- and if unrecoverable, restate the G38
ft/s figures as a range across 23.976-30 fps rather than a point value.
Bar unchanged; this restates a number, it does not fix a defect.
